## The context

Mathura Das Mathur Hospital approached us on the occasion of Women's Day. It wanted a newsletter to share among its 300+ staff, to instil a sense of pride, appreciation and solidarity among the women who keep the hospital running every day. It asked us for both visual design and copywriting.

## The challenge

A newsletter for a hospital has to be warm and trustworthy at once. The project required balancing several considerations:

- Establishing a coherent editorial direction around the publication's theme.
- Identifying relevant medical topics and informational material.
- Researching and verifying medical information before publication.
- Integrating content supplied by hospital management with independently developed editorial material.
- Translating complex medical information into accessible, well-structured content.
- Developing a professional visual identity appropriate for an institutional healthcare publication.

## What we did

We set the editorial direction first, so that every piece in the newsletter served the theme of recognising the women of the hospital.

We then chose the medical topics and informational material that would be useful to the readers. Each piece of medical information was researched and checked against reliable sources before it went into the newsletter.

We combined the content supplied by hospital management with the editorial material we developed ourselves, and edited both into a single voice. Complex medical information was rewritten in plain, well-structured language so that staff from every role could read and use it.

Finally, we developed a professional visual identity for the publication, with a design suited to an institutional healthcare publication, and laid out the newsletter for sharing across the hospital.

## The outcome

The hospital received a finished, professionally designed newsletter that it could share with its 300+ staff. It brought management's own content and verified medical information together in one publication, and gave the hospital a way to recognise the women who keep it running every day.
